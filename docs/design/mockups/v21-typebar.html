What the re-skin changes, and why each one.

1. Coral is gone, twice. V1's coralStrong filled the chosen alignment and both toggles when on, under a hardcoded Color.White that never flipped with the theme. V2.1's chosen-state is --leaf under --on-leaf, which does. Separately, the Coral swatch was painted through the themed coralText lookup on the one control that must not theme; it now draws its committed value like the other four.
2. The card joins the corpus. --menu ground, --fieldEdge hairline and a 6 dp soft elevation became .inkpop's card: --surface, a 1.5 px --ink edge, --br-lg, and the flat 4 px offset shadow. V2.1 has no blurred shadow anywhere; a soft one is a V1 tell as reliable as coral.
3. Every --field fill became --surface. The controls inside a chrome card share its themed surface, separated by their ink edges — the corpus's mechanism for separating a control from its ground, and the reason it needs no second fill token.
4. The joined segment became .paperseg. Three separate buttons on a gap, because that is what V2.1 has for a spelled-out choice of N and it costs one rule instead of two plus a divider.
5. The swatch's selected ring became .pot's dashed outer ring. Same hand as the selection ring on the page, meaning the same thing, and it no longer eats 2 px of the swatch's own colour to say it.
6. The row labels became .inklbl. Same four strings, the corpus's treatment for a label inside a floating card.

What this file deliberately does NOT change. The first three rows' shared right edge; the ten-stop ramp and its ends; the 400 ms settle that coalesces a burst into one undo entry while the canvas repaints on every step; the absence of any commit, cancel or close control, because this bar owns no state and cancel is undo; the 46 px control floor and the 58 px readout box, both of which the shared right edge is measured from; the radio-group and checkbox semantics ADR-055 §4 chose over the frozen HTML's aria-pressed triple; the readout's own name, which §5 chose over the frozen aria-hidden; the five ink values, which are printed ink and theme-independent by ADR-055 Decision 6; and the haptic on every accepted change, which no HTML prototype can express and which rememberStyleBuzz already ships — see ADR-102 §12.15 on why a freeze can only specify what its medium can hold.

Two questions this file does not answer, because they are the owner's.
1. How the panel closes. Both questions now have researched answers. Neither is taken; both are recommended. Evidence and citations: docs/BETA-UX-REVIEW.md F-14 and the 2026-08-15 research pass.

1. How the panel closes — the fix is three behaviours, and none of them is a button. The strongest finding is that the affordance is not the problem; the word "Done" is. GNOME's HIG draws the line exactly where this file needs it: instant-apply surfaces get a plain close control, and only explicit-apply surfaces get Done + Cancel — where Cancel resets to the pre-open state. This panel is instant-apply and its cancel is Undo, so a "Done" would advertise a commit model it does not have. That is the trap to avoid, and it is worth stating because the shipped bottom bar's Done is currently what closes this panel by side effect.
Recommended, in order, and all three are behaviour rather than visual design — so none of them reopens a freeze:
  (a) Register BackHandler. One line. The corpus's other overlays (pageGridOpen, inkPopoverOpen) already do; this one does not. The internal inconsistency is the sharper argument than any external guideline — and no platform document actually states "Back must dismiss a non-modal overlay", so this rests on Material's own standard sheets consuming Back once integrated, TalkBack mapping swipe-down-then-left to Back, and this app's own precedent. Three converging practical signals, not a written rule. It also matters most for accessibility: if Back does nothing here, a screen-reader user's one canonical "get me out" gesture fails silently.
  (b) Tap-outside closes the panel before it deselects. This is the documented behaviour of both closest analogues — Procreate ("tap off your text… to close the Edit Style panel") and Keynote iOS ("to close the text formatting controls, tap outside the controls"). Closing before deselecting preserves the selection, which is what a maker wants when they put a style panel away mid-edit.
  (c) A CustomAccessibilityAction, "Close type options." Zero pixels, and it is Android's own documented answer for a dismissal that otherwise depends on an untargeted spatial gesture. Neither Back nor tap-outside fully covers Switch Access, which only reaches focusable nodes — there is no "empty canvas" for it to tap.
On a visible close control — the one place this file's situation genuinely differs. Procreate and Keynote both keep a visible anchor on screen; this panel does not, because the context bar is withheld. Canva is the one surveyed app that likewise replaces its toolbar, and it is also the one that ships an explicit close icon. That is probably not a coincidence, and it means the Procreate/Keynote precedent does not rebut adding one here. Recommendation: ship (a)–(c) first and re-run a first-time pass. If people still hunt for the exit, add a chevron labelled Close — never Done. A visible affordance is the fix for a demonstrated discoverability failure, and it is the only one of these options that costs a design change.

2. The word "Coral" — rename it to Brick, on a measurement rather than on taste. Measuring both vocabularies as text against both papers settles it: not one Bench maker ink meets AA at normal text size, and every Type bar value is a darkened form of one that does. The two sets are not rivals — one is the other, corrected. Two of the five already keep the Bench name (Ink, Ochre), so the convention exists and was simply applied to two swatches and not the other three. Coral #A63C22 measures 5.96 / 5.28 and is the contrast-corrected form of Bench Brick #B0503F, which measures 4.82 / 4.27 — failing AA on Cream by a hair, exactly the near-miss that earns a darkened twin. So the rename does not invent a name; it restores the one the convention already implies, and retiring the abolished word falls out as a consequence rather than being the goal.
The rule this file should freeze, which is worth more than the rename: a text ink carries the NAME of the Bench maker ink it corrects, and a darker VALUE chosen to clear AA as normal text on both papers. The name names the material; the value serves legibility. Under it, Teal→Aqua and Blue→Cornflower follow — held back deliberately so the renames land once, under a stated convention, rather than twice.
A defect found while answering this, which must not be bundled with a rename. Teal #2A9D8F measures 3.10 / 2.75 — it fails AA as text on both papers, clearing AA-Large on --paper by 0.10 and failing even that on Cream. It is the one member of a set whose entire justification is contrast correction that was never actually corrected. That needs a corrected value, measured on device, and it is an accessibility fix rather than a naming one.

A36's rules. Cite them as A36.1 to A36.5.

  1. A row that does not fit puts its choices under its label. When a labelled row's label and choices do not fit side by side in the card, the choices move to a second line, starting at the left, 4 px (--gap-xs) below the label. This is the Colour row's shape, used only when it is needed. It applies to Font, Size, Align and Style alike.
  2. Where a row fits, nothing moves. The card is still as wide as its widest row laid out on one line, up to its width limit, so the rows only stack once the card has reached that limit. At usual text no row stacks, and no drawing above this block changes.
  3. A word on a choice never breaks inside itself. "Left", "Center" and "Right" each stay on one line at every text size. A29's large-text phone drew them broken on purpose, to show the app's defect of 2026-10-07; it now draws the stacked row instead.
  4. Only the row that does not fit stacks. The others keep their label at the left and their choices at the right, so at the largest text the card mixes two shapes. On a 360 dp phone at font scale 2.0 the app stacks only Align. On a first reading this looked like a different kind of row, not a broken one, and every word was whole (D-115). On A29's phone above, with the name "Lettering" (which A29's V-1 did not choose), the Font row stacks too, so the cut tick and shadow that A29's notes describe for that name no longer appear.
  5. What a screen reader hears does not change. Each row's label still names its choices, and it is still read first. Every choice keeps its 48 dp target. No word is added or changed.

A29H's rules. Cite them as A29H.1 to A29H.12.

  1. The Font row has three words, in this order: Book, Plain, Hand. Hand is added at the end so the two words a maker already knows keep their place. Still one choice of three, a single-choice group named by its label. The row's name stays "Font".
  2. "Hand" is written in Shantell Sans. A29.2 wrote each word in a face the interface already had and loaded no document font into it. The interface has no hand face, and one it does draw in (Averia) is not the one the maker would get. So the word is a sample of the choice, which is the reason V-4 gave for Book, and the app draws it with the same bundled Regular file the editing field already draws a Hand text with. No new file is loaded into the interface. This is the only word in the interface set in a document font.
  3. The clothes are A29.3's and the corner tick is A29.4's, unchanged. A touch is at least 48 dp tall and wide.
  4. The row is no wider than Align. Three words in the Font row fit where Align's three do, so the card's width is unchanged. At the largest text the row stacks under its label exactly as A36 rules for Align, and no word breaks (A29.5, A36.3).
  5. Hand sets Latin letters and nothing else, as Book does. It has no Greek and no Cyrillic. A word that cannot be used is drawn and spoken as A29.7 rules, still enabled, still tappable, and a tap changes nothing.
  6. Hand's smallest size is 12 pt, a value of its own that today equals Book's. It is not borrowed: the printed page may move one without the other. Measured on the face, Hand's x-height at 10 pt is 1.70 mm against Fraunces 1.66 mm and Inter 1.93 mm, so 12 pt is the same standard of legibility Book was ruled at, and it is to be confirmed on the printed page. Nothing changes a size or a font for the maker.
  7. One reason line, still directly under the Font row, and it names every word it speaks for. The order is A29.8's: the unknown font, then the script, then the size. When both Book and Hand are quiet for the same cause the line names both: "Book and Hand have no Greek letters, so this text stays Plain." · "Book and Hand need 12 pt or larger. Make the text larger first." When only one is quiet it names that one: "Hand has no Greek letters, so this text stays Book." · "Hand needs 12 pt or larger. Make the text larger first." A tap on one quiet word says the line for that word alone. The word after "stays" is the font the text is in.
  8. Smaller, on a Hand text at 12 pt, is drawn like A29.9 and the line is "Hand stops at 12 pt. Switch to Plain to go smaller." The real bottom of the ramp (10 pt) stays truly unavailable.
  9. A font this app does not know: no word is chosen, and the line becomes "This text uses a font this version of Zinely does not have. It is drawn in Plain for now. Choosing Book, Plain or Hand replaces its font."
  10. Typing a letter Hand does not have into a Hand text raises the typing-time notice, naming the font: "Hand has no Greek letters. Switch this text to Plain to print them in a matching face." The font is never switched for the maker.
  11. A change of font can push words out of the box, as A29.12 says, and Hand is the widest of the three (about 9 % wider than Plain in running text, and its line is 1.34 of the size against Plain's 1.21). A29H adds no sign for it. Going the other way is safe: a build that does not have Hand draws it in Plain, which is narrower and shorter, so nothing is cut off there. The older build shows the words it can and says nothing.
  12. Undo and Redo say "Font put back" and "Font changed" for a Hand change, as A30 rules. The saved format does not change: a Hand text holds the family name "Shantell Sans" in the same string every font uses.
