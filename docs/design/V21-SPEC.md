## 1. What V2.1 is, in one paragraph

V2.1 is **V2 with its volume turned up**. It keeps everything V2 got right — the paper metaphor, the
privacy-first voice, the one-question-per-screen structure, the four-state discipline, the riso grain, the
warm ground and soft brown-black ink — and changes four things: **depth becomes hard and printed** instead of
soft and blurred, **geometry gains a published scale** and things at rest sit at an angle, **a deliberately
imperfect display face** carries the voice, and **craft motifs** (tape, stamps, seals, a shelf lip) appear
where the screen is about material.

🟨 **ASSUMPTION, stated plainly:** this is a re-skin, not a re-architecture. No screen changes the question it
answers, no flow gains or loses a step, and no privacy or offline invariant moves. If any of that turns out to
be false in implementation, it is a defect in this document, not a licence to change scope.

## 3. The four rulings this document asks for

These are the questions [V21-RESEARCH.md §6](V21-RESEARCH.md) raised and refused to answer. Each carries a
recommendation and the reasoning against it, so the owner can overrule on the evidence rather than on my

### 3.1 The display face — 🟦 **RECOMMENDATION: bundle Averia Sans Libre**

**The case for.** The handmade read does not survive without it. Fraunces is a refined contemporary serif; it
can be *warm*, but it cannot be *wonky*, and the wobble is the entire difference between "tasteful" and
"made by a person". Averia Sans Libre is generated by averaging handwriting samples — the imperfection is
structural, not a filter. It is OFL-licensed, free to bundle and to modify.

**The case against, honestly.** It is a **fourth bundled family** (Inter + Fraunces + Averia), and it costs
APK size and Latin-only coverage.

**The recommendation, with a condition.** Bundle it — but **subset it**. It is used only for headings, labels
and short display strings, never for body text, so a Latin-basic + punctuation subset is sufficient. Body text
stays Inter; long-form editorial stays Fraunces.

#### ✅ VERIFIED — measured 2026-08-09, and the condition turns out to be moot

Measured on the bytes the three prototypes actually inline, subset with `fontTools.subset` 4.63 to
Latin-basic + Latin-1 + typographic punctuation, layout features `kern,liga,ccmp,locl,mark,mkmk`. **Android
bundles TTF in `res/font`** (there is no woff2 path), so the shipping figure is TTF and the APK figure is that
TTF deflated:

| Variant | glyphs | TTF, both weights | **deflated in the APK** | woff2, both weights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Upstream OFL release — WHAT SHIPS** | **234** | **216.1 KB** | **123.6 KB, measured in the built APK** | — |
| Full, as the prototypes inline it | 234 | 207.8 KB | 116.0 KB | 74.1 KB |
| Subset Latin + punctuation | 208 | 192.9 KB | 108.3 KB | 69.8 KB |
| Subset ASCII only | 96 | 58.2 KB | 37.1 KB | 26.5 KB |

⚠️ **The first row was added on 2026-08-10 and it corrects the rest.** The measurement below was taken from the
bytes the prototypes inline, which are Google Fonts' *served* build. Compose bundles the **upstream OFL release**
(`google/fonts@main/ofl/averiasanslibre`), which carries 231 codepoints to the served build's 213. The caveat at
the foot of this section demanded a re-measure once a real implementation existed; it has now been taken **from
the built APK** rather than estimated:

```
res/font/averia_sans_libre_regular.ttf   105.8 KB raw ->  59.6 KB in-APK
res/font/averia_sans_libre_bold.ttf      110.3 KB raw ->  62.0 KB in-APK
                                                          123.6 KB
```

**123.6 KB is the true cost** — 7.6 KB above the 116.0 KB this section originally reported, and 1.7 KB above the
121.9 KB a local `zlib` estimate gave, because AAPT's deflate is not `zlib.compress(level=9)`. *Estimating a
compressed size twice produced two wrong numbers; the APK produced the right one.*

🔭 **Unrelated observation, recorded because the same measurement surfaced it.** Inter ships **twice** — four
cuts in `res/font/` (chrome) and four in `assets/fonts/` (the render engine's document fonts) — for **~790 KB of
duplicated bytes in the APK**, six times what this whole typography decision costs. Not V2.1's to fix, and worth
someone's attention.

Three findings, all of which correct this section rather than confirm it:

1. **The "≈ 101 KB woff2" figure was wrong.** 101.8 KB is the *TTF* size of the *400 weight alone*. The pair
   is **74.1 KB** as woff2 and **207.8 KB** as the TTF Android ships.
2. **Subsetting buys 7.7 KB of APK, and is not worth doing.** The inlined face is already a Latin subset —
   213 codepoints, ASCII 95/95, Latin-1 96/96, Latin Extended-A 3/128. There is nothing material left to cut,
   so *"bundle it but subset it"* is satisfiable and pointless.
3. **The only real lever is coverage or weight count.** ASCII-only reaches 37.1 KB but loses every accented
   character, and the voice face sets zine titles the user types — so it is not available. One weight instead
   of two roughly halves the cost. **The honest price of this decision is ~116 KB of APK, and it does not
   shrink.**

⚠️ Measured on the prototype's inlined bytes. A Compose implementation that pulls the upstream OFL release
starts from a different glyph set and must re-measure.

### 3.2 The fourth hue — 🟦 **RECOMMENDATION: keep the rule; butter is not chrome**

[V2-TOKENS.md](V2-TOKENS.md) says: *"Two brand hues + one consequence colour are the whole chrome palette…
**No fourth chrome hue.**"* V2.1 adds **butter**. That looks like a violation, and the temptation is to ask
the owner to weaken the rule. **Don't.**

Read what the rule is actually protecting: *role* clarity. Matcha is the single "your move", strawberry is
sparing punctuation, consequence is the only urgent colour. The rule exists so a user never has to ask what a
colour means. Butter takes **none** of those roles:

| | Carries an action? | Carries a state? | Carries text? | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **leaf** (= matcha) | ✅ the only one | — | ✅ | your next move |
| **berry** (= strawberry) | ❌ never | ✅ current-page, stamps | ✅ deep cut only | punctuation |
| **jam** (= consequence) | delete only | ✅ error | ✅ | the only urgent colour |
| **butter** | ❌ **never** | ❌ **never alone** | ❌ **never** | **material** — tape, the shelf lip, the ring around the primary, a caution ground |

⚠️ **"never *alone*" is exact, and the word is load-bearing.** The Bench's favourite star is a butter fill
inside an **ink outline**: the ink boundary is what carries the state and what clears
[1.4.11](#41-colour) — butter alone on paper is 1.73:1 (§4.1 row 4). Read as an absolute, this row invites a
future reader to "restore the rule" by deleting the outline, which would re-break it.

So the correct resolution is not a fourth *chrome* hue but a **second material category**, which V2-TOKENS
already establishes a precedent for: *"Cover inks (the maker's palette, **not the chrome**)"*. Butter joins
that side of the line. **The no-fourth-chrome-hue rule survives V2.1 unchanged, and the two ADRs and the lint
wall that depend on it are undisturbed.**

**This was not free.** Writing this section falsified two of my own prototypes and both were fixed rather than
argued away:

- Proof: **"Fold it up"** was butter. It is the user's next move, so it is now **leaf**.
- Bench: the **current page** in the filmstrip was ringed butter. That is a state, and V2-TOKENS assigns the
  current-page dot to strawberry, so it is now **berry**.

**The one deliberate exception has since been retired — measurement killed it.** The snackbar's **Undo** was
left butter on the ink ground, on the reasoning that neither leaf nor berry clears contrast there. That
reasoning was done in light theme only: in dark, `ink` becomes `#F6EAD6` and butter-on-ink collapses to
**1.59:1** (§4.1). Undo is now the snackbar's own `paper` colour, underlined — 13.66 / 11.70, and **butter
touches no action anywhere in the corpus.** The rule in the table above is now literally true, with no
exception attached to it.

**A second violation of the same rule was found by the same measurement.** The Bench's favourite star
(`.fav.on`) carried butter as a **state**, at 1.73:1 on paper. It is now a butter fill inside an **ink
outline**: the ink boundary carries the state and clears 1.4.11, and butter is back to being the material
inside it.

🟦 **The binding specification is the intent, not the CSS.** The prototype expresses the outline with
`-webkit-text-stroke: .7px var(--ink)` plus `paint-order: stroke fill`, which is a vendor-prefixed browser
property with **no Compose equivalent to look up**. What Compose must implement is: **a butter fill with a
0.7 px ink outline, the stroke painted beneath the fill** — two `drawText` passes (`Stroke`, then `Fill`), or
an equivalent `drawWithContent` overlay. An implementation that reproduces the *intent* is correct even where
it shares no property name with the HTML.

### 3.3 Scales — 🟦 **RECOMMENDATION: publish them, because V2.1 authored them**

[D-006](../DECISIONS.md) ruled no V2 radius scale and [D-007](../DECISIONS.md) ruled spacing stays per
component *"exactly as frozen"*. **Neither ruling is being overturned, because neither was a preference.**
Both were findings *about the frozen corpus*: V2 had 16 distinct chrome radii and no scale, so there was
nothing to publish and inventing one would have been fabrication.

V2.1's corpus is different in the only way that matters — ~~it was **authored with a scale from the first
line**~~, following Maeve's `4/8/14/24/40` and `4/8/16/24/36` ([V21-RESEARCH §2.3](V21-RESEARCH.md)):

```
--br-xs:4px  --br-sm:8px  --br-md:14px  --br-lg:22px  --br-xl:36px  --br-pill:999px
```

> **The struck sentence was true of radius and false of spacing**, and the `--gap-*` line that used to sit in
> this block — `4/8/16/24/36` — was **declared in one prototype and used nowhere**. It is deleted rather than
> corrected, because the ladder the corpus actually needed turned out to be a different one; see the measured
> verdict below for the real `--gap-*` values. Keeping a superseded ladder here would leave two spacing scales
> in the specification, which is the failure mode this section exists to prevent.

So V2.1 **publishes both scales** — radius as authored, spacing as refitted below — and the reason D-006/D-007
said no still holds for V2: it is the corpus that changed, not the principle. 🟦 This also does real work
downstream: it is the difference between "route every value through a token" being a slogan and being
mechanically checkable.

#### ⚠️ MEASURED 2026-08-09 — half of this section is true, and the half that is not is the half that matters

An independent review challenged the premise — *"authored with a scale from the first line"* — and it does not
survive counting:

| | `--br-*` defined | `--br-*` used | `--gap-*` defined | `--gap-*` used | spacing literals on the published 4/8/16/24/36 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Library | 6 | **23** | 5 | **0** | 23.0% |
| Proof | 6 | **35** | **0** | **0** | 20.8% |
| Bench | 6 | **46** | **0** | **0** | 25.8% |

`--hard` is used 6/12/6 and `--frame` 2/2/2. **The radius scale is real** — declared in all three files, used
104 times, and it earns publication on exactly the evidence this section claims.

**The spacing scale is not.** It is declared in one prototype, used **nowhere**, and the corpus spends 7, 9,
11, 13 and 17 px freely. Worse, the number that decides it is the number [D-007](../DECISIONS.md) already
ruled on: D-007 (**Accepted**) declined to publish a V2 spacing scale because the frozen CSS was **16.7%**
on-grid — *"an aspiration, not a token inventory"*. V2.1 measures **20.8–25.8%**. That is the same condition,
so publishing `--gap-*` would be the very fabrication D-006/D-007 refused, and this section's own argument
(*the corpus changed*) is the argument **against** it.

#### ✅ CLOSED 2026-08-09 — the claim was made true rather than withdrawn (owner ruling)

Fitting confirmed there was nothing latent to discover: the brute-forced best-fit 7-step ladder for this
corpus is `2/5/8/11/14/18/28`, which is a description of noise. So the choice was withdraw the scale or
**author it**, and the owner ruled: author it ([V21-RESEARCH §8.3](V21-RESEARCH.md)).

```
--gap-hair:2px  --gap-xs:4px  --gap-sm:8px  --gap-md:12px
--gap-lg:16px   --gap-xl:24px --gap-2xl:36px
```

Maeve's ladder, plus `12` where the corpus genuinely clustered and a `2 px` hairline step it could not do
without. **273 spacing declarations across all three prototypes now route through it** — mean movement
**0.94 px**, **97.1% within 2 px**, worst case 4 px on seven declarations. Values above 40 px are one-off
layout dimensions rather than rhythm (`60`, `64`, `132`, `150`) and stay literal by design.

**The honest label for this is a refit, not a discovery.** §3.3 originally said the corpus *"was authored with
a scale from the first line"*; it was not, and that sentence was false when written. It is true now because
the corpus was moved to match it — which is a legitimate thing to do to an unfrozen prototype, and a very
different claim from the one this section made.

The radius scale needed no such work: `--br-*` was declared in all three files and used **104 times** before
any of this, and `--hard` 24 times, `--frame` 6. **That half of §3.3 was always sound.**

#### The ladder against V2-CONSTITUTION §III's 8pt rhythm — ✅ **CLOSED 2026-08-10**

**Owner ruling: the rhythm is a generosity principle, not a modular grid, so the ladder never conflicted with
it.** The evidence is the clause's own corpus — [D-007](../DECISIONS.md) measured the V2 trilogy, ratified
under that constitution, at **16.7% on-grid**, and was Accepted on that finding. A clause its own design
violated in five declarations out of six was never operating as a grid. What it governs is what it says:
*spacing is calm and generous; density is never used to fit more chrome.* The ladder passes that at every
step. **The layout/inset split is withdrawn, not corrected** — it answered a question that did not exist.
Implementation transcribes the corpus value at each address.

The falsified reasoning is kept below, because the failure is the instructive part.

#### ~~The ladder against the 8pt rhythm — REOPENED~~ *(superseded 2026-08-10)*

Four steps — `2, 4, 12, 36` — are off an 8pt grid, and the Constitution says an **8pt rhythm governs layout**.
A second review raised the conflict; the owner ruled the narrowest of three readings: **the rhythm binds
*layout* and never bound sub-component insets.** That ruling stands.

**The mapping under it did not.** This section, the Constitution's note and `ZinelyV21Dimens` all said
`gap-hair / gap-xs / gap-md` were inside-a-control values and out of scope — and a third review found
`--gap-md` spacing **layout** throughout the frozen corpus: `.pgg` (a page grid) and `.canvasArea` in the
Bench, `.band`, `.dbody` and `.testcard` in the Proof, `.empty` and `.sh-head` in the Library, among ~59
declarations. It is the corpus's most-used step. That classification was the implementer's, not the owner's,
and it was written as though it were the owner's.

So under the ruling as written, the frozen corpus is off the 8pt rhythm in ~59 places, and the three
readings are live again with evidence under them — §8 row 11, [V2-CONSTITUTION §III](V2-CONSTITUTION.md#spacing).
**Binding until answered:** transcribe the corpus value at each address. Substituting a 16 for a corpus 12 to
make a surface grid-clean would settle a constitutional question with a padding value.

Consequence for Phase D, now discharged: [ADR-099 §1](../DECISIONS.md#adr-099) argues the 79 unsourced
literals *"get a destination for the first time"* from the published scales. With both scales real and used,
**both radii and spacing now have one.**

### 3.4 Scope — 🟦 **RECOMMENDATION: all three surfaces, and no more**

The Library alone would leave the app speaking two languages at the exact moment a beta tester moves between
screens. All three are built and accepted, so freeze all three. **Do not extend the freeze to Read, Fold or
first-run** — they have no prototype, and freezing a surface nobody has drawn is how a spec starts lying.

- `inkSoft` on `deskEdge` is **4.49** against 4.50, and it is **not a live pairing**. `deskEdge` grounds only
  the keyboard body; the keys themselves sit on `paper`. Recorded so a future screen does not put text there.
- `jam` on `paper` at **4.20** survives in one place, `v21-library.html .fail .mk` at 28.8 px bold — large
  text, which needs 3:1. Kept deliberately: at display size the consequence colour should be the bright one.
- **`inkFaint` strokes clear 1.4.11 by 0.04.** The fold guide's `.crease` and `.ghost`, the Bench's search

  them moving onto `bench` fails** (`.search svg` would be 2.30). §5.3 makes the crease/fold/cut legend

- **`.cover .mark` keeps a hardcoded `rgba(255,246,232,.92)` on purpose.** Cover art is the maker's palette,
  not the chrome, and it is theme-invariant by design ([V2-TOKENS.md](V2-TOKENS.md) draws exactly this line).
  Stated so the next literal sweep does not re-flag it as a third instance of rows 6–7.

**One further finding, researched and closed 2026-08-09 — see §4.3, *a border is not a shadow*.** `inkLine` is
`#120E0A` in dark, deliberately near-black because a printed shadow must be darker than the paper it falls on.
The corpus also routed **61 borders** through it (8 Library · 19 Proof · 34 Bench), putting them at **1.38:1
against `paper` and 1.17:1 against `desk`** in dark, where `paper` on `desk` is itself only 1.18. All 61 now
follow `ink`: **11.70 / 13.86 in dark, and byte-identical in light**, because `ink` and `inkLine` are the same
`#33261C` there.

> **The count was reported as 59 until a review re-derived it.** 59 was the number of *replacements the edit
> made*; two Bench borders were already on `ink` and needed none. The corpus **state** is 61 borders on `ink`,
> and a state is what a specification should record. `inkLine` keeps **56** uses: 55 hard-shadow declarations
> plus one deliberate border — see §4.3.

⚠️ **Recorded because it would be easy to claim more than the evidence supports:** this was **not** a WCAG
failure. [1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast) binds *"visual information required to
identify **user interface components**"*, and a decorative card frame is not a control
([V21-RESEARCH §8.1](V21-RESEARCH.md)). The borders were repainted for **design consistency with the
language's own rule** — §4.1 says `ink` carries *"every border and hard shadow"* — not for compliance. The
one case that *was* unambiguously binding, the fold guide's own outline, is §4.1 row 8.

Finding 1 is the one worth remembering: **the prototypes broke this section's own rule** — *"`inkFaint` …
never body text"* — across roughly two thirds of the Bench's and the Proof's small text. The measurement did
not find a palette that was too dark; it found a palette being used against its own specification.

### 4.2 Type

| Role | Face | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | Averia Sans Libre 400/700 | headings, screen titles, the maker's own words on a page |
| **Editorial** | Fraunces 400/500, italic 400 | long-form zine body, captions, pull quotes, guide prose |
| **Work** | Inter 400/500/600/700 | every control, label, chip, number and piece of chrome |

The rule that keeps this from reading as amateur is the reference sites' own: **the imperfect face never sets
running text.** Voice is for short strings; Work is for anything the user operates.

### 4.3 Depth, geometry, motion

- **`--hard: 4px`** — the signature move. An offset shadow in `inkLine`, **zero blur**, always down-right,
  always the full shadow colour. It is a *printed* shadow, not elevation: the object physically moves under
  your finger.

  **Four press tiers.** The corpus has **18 `:active` rule blocks / 19 selectors**, of which **17 carry
  depth**; the one excluded is `.act:active`, which changes background only and has no shadow at rest. This
  section used to state the hero case alone (*"a pressed control translates `2px, 2px` and drops its shadow
  to `1px`"*) as though it governed everything, and the Compose layer published it that way before the rules
  were counted:

  | Tier | Rest | Travel | Pressed | Where |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Hero** | `--hard` 4 | 2 | **1** | `.cover` (every Library cover) · `.start` · `.btn-save` · `.btn-share` · `.add` |
  | **Raised** | 3 | 2 | 1 | `.retry` · `.foldit` · `.shelf` · `.opt` · `.paperseg button` |
  | **Flat** | 2 | 2 | **0** | `.chip` · `.tile` · `.iconbtn` · `.icon-btn` · `.fnav` — presses flush |
  | **Inline** | 2 | 1 | 1 | `.ctl`, in all three prototypes — a control inside another surface |

  Three follow *pressed = rest − travel*; **Hero does not**, travelling 2 and shedding 3. This document
  briefly explained that as a deliberate expressive choice for "the one primary action on a screen". **Both
  halves of that were wrong**, and a review caught them:

  - Every Hero *and* Raised `:active` rule writes the byte-identical `translate(2px,2px)` +
    `box-shadow: 1px 1px 0`, across nine selectors and three files. What the corpus shows is **one pressed
    value of 1dp reused regardless of rest depth** — not a per-tier decision. The reason is unknown, and the
    numbers are transcribed because the HTML is canonical, not because the story was convincing.
  - Hero is **not** one-per-screen: `.cover` wears it on every tile in the Library grid, and the Proof puts
    `.btn-save` and `.btn-share` at Hero together. One-per-screen belongs to the **`--frame` ring**, which
    appears exactly twice per file — a base rule and its `:active`.

  It stays a table rather than a formula regardless: a formula would silently move whichever tier disagreed
  with it. Only `4` is tokenised; `3` and `2` are literals at their use sites.
- **A border is not a shadow, and they do not share a token.** A **drawn line** — every border, outline and
  SVG stroke — is *ink*, and follows `ink`. A **shadow** is the absence of light, must stay darker than the
  surface it falls on, and follows `inkLine` (`#120E0A` in dark). They are byte-identical in light theme
  (`#33261C`) and must diverge in dark; collapsing them cost 61 near-invisible borders and one near-invisible
  fold guide (§4.1 row 8, [V21-RESEARCH §8.2](V21-RESEARCH.md), where the reference category does the same).
  **The exceptions are elements whose own ground is `ink`**, where an ink border would be invisible and the
  shadow ink is correct: the Bench's snackbar and the Proof's flash toast — the same pattern, resolved the
  same way. The rule is *a border contrasts with what it sits on*, not *a border is always ink*.
  (A third ink-on-ink pair, the Bench's `.chip .sw`, is a colour **swatch**: its fill is the maker's runtime
  ink, and its ring separates it from the chip's `paper` ground, so `ink` is correct there. This spec said
  "the single exception" until a review found all three — the blanket repaint had made border == background,
  and only the snackbar was caught by the first scan, which matched one rule per selector.)
- **`--frame: 5px`** — a flat colour ring, `0 0 0 var(--frame)`, stacked outside a hard shadow. Reserved for
  **the one primary action on a screen** (Save PDF, Add). Riso misregistration, borrowed from Maeve.
- **Radius / spacing** — the published scales in §3.3. Pills for controls, `--br-md`/`--br-lg` for cards and
  drawers, `--br-xl` for a bottom sheet's top corners.
- **Tilt** — objects **at rest** sit at ±0.6–2°. Objects **being worked on** never tilt (see §5.2).

  **The corpus phases tilt and tape in a three-cycle keyed to the object's index among its own kind.** The
  frozen file expressed this as `:nth-child(3n+k)` and was **off by one**, because a `.shelf-head` and four
  `.ph` placeholders share the grid and `:nth-child` counts them. Corrected to `:nth-of-type` on 2026-08-11
  — three tilt rules and two tape rules — per [ADR-100 §5](../DECISIONS.md#adr-100-nth-child), which is
  also why the tile is an `<article>`: `.shelf-head` and `.ph` are `<div>`s, so a `<div>` tile would
  reintroduce the same defect. This is the one place a specification was moved to agree with an
  implementation, and it is allowed because the file was wrong.
- **An overflow affordance is a drawn mark, never a character, and it is vertical.** Three dots, 3dp across
  with 3dp gaps, stacked, in `inkSoft`, inside a 34dp target that is a **sibling** of the tile's own button,
  not a child of it — a control may not contain a control, which is why `.zine` is an `<article>` holding
  `.zine-open` and `.more` rather than the `<button>` it was. Reasoning:
  [ADR-100 §1](../DECISIONS.md#adr-100-overflow).
- **A zine's title on the shelf caps at two lines, with an end ellipsis.** Uniform across every tile and
  across every font scale, so the grid stays rigid: two equal-height columns mean an unbounded title in one
  cell pushes its **neighbour's** cover down. The full title is reachable from the `.more` sheet's header,
  which sets no cap. Reasoning, alternatives and the measurement that ruled out middle-ellipsis:
  [ADR-100 §3](../DECISIONS.md#adr-100-long-titles).
- **Grain** — an SVG turbulence overlay, `soft-light` over chrome and `multiply` over paper.
- **Motion** stays V2's: short, `cubic-bezier(.05,.7,.1,1)` for entrances, ≤ 300 ms. `prefers-reduced-motion`
  **downgrades** motion; it never removes the state change that motion was communicating.

## 5. The two rules the prototypes discovered

Both came out of building, not out of research, and both are binding.

### 5.1 Craft belongs to material, not to tools *(the Bench rule)*

An editor may not be charming at the cost of being precise. The voice is spent where a screen is about
**material** — the page, the shelf, the ink pots, the supply drawer — and withheld where it is about
**accuracy**:

- **The page never tilts.** You are working on it, so it sits square to you. Tilt is for objects at rest.
- **The contextual verb bar is flat and quiet** — no offset shadow, no tape, no rotation. A tool that performs
  is a tool you stop trusting.
- **Selection is a hand-drawn dashed ring in ink**, not a system blue box.

### 5.2 Physical honesty beats visual convenience *(the Proof rule)*

An eight-page one-sheet zine reads as cover alone · 2|3 · 4|5 · 6|7 · back alone. V2.1 renders that model
even though only **one leaf is on screen at a time**: the spine lands on the physically correct side of each
page (2 binds right, 3 binds left), the gutter shades that edge, and the closed remainder of the book shows as
a stack edge — so you feel where you are without ever seeing two unreadable pages.

**A side-by-side spread was built and rejected** (owner, 2026-08-09): two mini-zine pages at 390 px are two
pages you cannot read. 🔭 A tablet spread is a separate scope decision, not a free consequence of this HTML.

The same rule produced the rebuilt fold guide: **eight steps, each exactly one physical action**, drawn on the
sheet the printer actually produced, with a legend that never changes meaning — grey dashed = a crease you
already made · green = the fold you are making now · red = the cut — and a line per step saying what you should
be **holding** when it is done.

### 5.3 Fold arrows follow origami's own conventions, not ours

✅ **VERIFIED.** The first fold guide's arrows were rejected by the owner as *messy* (2026-08-09). The cause
was not styling. Origami diagramming has a century-old standard — the **Yoshizawa–Randlett system**, set out
in detail by [Robert Lang](https://langorigami.com/article/origami-diagramming-conventions/) — and it names
this exact defect:

> *"The tail of an arrow of motion is often as important as the head… the tail indicates precisely the
> reference point or spot undergoing motion. If you are to bring point A to point B, the tail of the arrow
> will touch [A]."*

Every arrow in the first version **floated beside the sheet, touching no paper.** Its tail named no reference
point, so it decorated the step instead of instructing it. The three rules now binding on any Zinely fold or
assembly diagram:

| Arrow | Stem | Means | Used at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motion** | plain single line, **no tail decoration**, filled symmetric head | *this paper travels* — tail on the flap that moves, head where it lands | steps 1, 2, 3, 6, 8 |
| **Action** | **hollow** stem | *do this to the paper* — never confusable with motion | push (step 7) |
| **Unfold** | hollow stem, **head at both ends** | *open it back out* | step 4 |
| *(none)* | — | **cutting carries no arrow** — only the blade, on the line | step 5 |

Head geometry is identical at every step. 🟦 This is worth adopting wholesale rather than reinventing: the
convention is older than the app, internationally understood, and already solved the problem we hit.
