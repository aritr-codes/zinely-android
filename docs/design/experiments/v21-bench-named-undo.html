Named undo — the undo snack amendment (A26)

Step 3 of the 1.x plan (Brief 04 A3, gate 3). After an undo, the Bench’s existing support-paper snack says what came back; after a redo, what was redone — and the page, when either takes you to another page. One snack, no button, spoken once. The owner ruled the four decisions and the Across-fold words on PR #88; this page shows the frozen design for the owner’s final look before the PR merges. Implementation is a separate session. Base origin/main a6eb16b, branch design/step3-undo-snack.

Every phone below is the real v21-bench.html staged by its ?a26= review stages; each shows the lower half of the phone at 1:1. In the live prototype, delete, flip and duplicate really undo with their named line: select something, act, then press the snack’s Undo or the bar’s Undo. Undo after Add Art or Across fold shows the words but reverts nothing, as the frozen prototype already did; ink is not demonstrated live. The prototype undo is one level deep and its Redo stays drawn disabled, so redo lines are staged in the same snack.

What stays exactly as frozen

  • The snack itself: pill, support-paper ground, ink border, .79rem type, the −0.6° tilt, 88px above the phone bottom (A8: 12px above a live context bar), the 180ms entrance, the 3200ms dwell. Nothing is restyled.
  • One slot. A new line replaces the last; there is never a second snack or a second speaker.
  • Undo is a snack, not a dialogue. An undo or redo snack carries no button.
  • Forward snacks (“Removed · …”, “Duplicated”, “Flipped …”, “Placed on the page”) are unchanged and keep their Undo.
  • No schema change, no persisted label; History and committing() unchanged.

What changed: the pre-A26 post-undo lines — “Put back” after a delete (:810) and after a flip (:1068), and “Duplicate undone” (:2216) — stay in the file as history, and A26 says a named line instead. Before A26 an undo after a placement, a spread or an ink also said “Put back”, which put nothing back.

A · The words

Paper words: the thing, then what happened to it. No coordinates, no command names, never the bare word “Undo”. The thing is Text, Photo, Art piece, or 3 things when one act held several. Undo says what came back (put back, taken off, … back); redo says the act again in forward words (removed, added, moved, changed …).

Rules that come with the table

  • Page clause. When an undo or redo lands on another page, the Bench goes there, clears the selection, and the same line gains , page N: Photo put back, page 3. One line, one snack, one announcement; it replaces today’s hard-coded “Changed page N”. A comma, not “·”, so the spoken line is the seen line.
  • Toggles name the state that came back: undoing Copier-on says Copier taken off; undoing Copier-off says Copier put back. Their redos name the state redone: Copier added / Copier removed. Flips likewise, per axis.
  • Words before style. One text edit that changed both the words and the style says Words put back.
  • Add Text is two undo steps. The first undo says Words taken off (the box stays, empty), the next Empty box taken off. Redo walks back: Empty box added, then Words added.
  • Replace on an Art piece also refits its box; the swap outranks the refit (Art piece swapped back).
  • Across fold redone says its frozen forward line, Photo runs across pages 2 & 3, which names its pages and so takes no page clause.
  • Restacking: an adjacent restack swaps two ranks, so “forward” and “back” are the same diff; one direction-free line fits. This rules the decision gate’s “restack direction” item.
  • Nothing left to undo or redo: no new snack.
  • Repeats: each undo or redo is a new snack instance (keyed per step), so a second identical line is meant to be spoken again. Whether TalkBack re-speaks it is checked on device, per Brief 04.
  • Names: things are named by kind, never by the maker’s words, so the “and N more” list convention (backup-restore.html) is untouched.
  • Not covered: adding or deleting a page. No Bench control does either.
  • VOICE.md’s “Undone. You can redo it.” is superseded by this table (updated in the same change).

F · Page-changing undo and redo

The photo was deleted on page 3; the maker went to page 6 and pressed Undo. The Bench returns to page 3 and says so in the same line — no second snack, no separate announcement. A redo that jumps page works the same way.

G · Repeated identical undo

Two undos that name the same thing are two snack instances: the text node is replaced and the frozen entrance restarts from hidden (live prototype: undo twice), so the second is meant to be spoken again — whether TalkBack re-speaks it is checked on device.
