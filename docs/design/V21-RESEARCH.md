## 2. What the three references actually declare

### 2.1 Nic's Ice Creams — ✅ VERIFIED

| | |
|---|---|
| Palette | `--color_primary:#512d1e` (deep cocoa brown) · `--color_sand_white:#f7e9d0` (cream ground) · `--color_secondary:#90d5ac` (soft mint) · `--color_red:#d84859` (berry) |
| Type | `--font_primary:"aesthet_nova_bold",serif` (display) + `--font_secondary:"Nunito",serif` (body — a rounded humanist sans) |
| Radius | `50px` and `50%` dominate — **pills and circles**, no small-radius scale |
| Shadow | effectively none (`box-shadow:none` is the only declaration found) |
| Craft signals | `texture` ×291, `tape` ×5, `torn` ×2, `handwrit` ×3, `grain` ×4, `sticker` ×1 |

**The finding:** a brown-on-cream ground with two bright accents, a quirky display serif over a
rounded sans, pill geometry, **no depth at all**, and texture doing the work depth would otherwise do.

### 2.2 The Belgian Waffle — ✅ VERIFIED

| | |
|---|---|
| Palette | `#FFFCD5` (pale butter ground) · `#FFC10E` (saturated yellow) · `#442619` / `#4D2D1E` (deep brown ink) · `#EC5934` (orange-red) |
| Type | `Familjen Grotesk` (body) · `Alkaline` and `BritanniaCGBold` (display) · **`Averia Sans Libre`** loaded from Google Fonts |
| Radius | `100px` pills, `50%`, plus a Tailwind `--radius-sm…4xl` ladder (`.25rem`→`2rem`) |
| Shadow | `10px -10px #ffffff80` — a **hard, un-blurred, offset** shadow |

**The finding, and it is the important one:** `Averia Sans Libre` is a typeface generated by
*averaging handwriting samples* — its wobble is the point. Pairing a deliberately imperfect display
face with a clean grotesk body is how this site reads handmade without looking amateur. And its one
shadow has **zero blur** — a printed offset, not elevation.

### 2.3 Maeve Chocolate — ✅ VERIFIED

The most systematic of the three, and the closest thing to a token set:

```
--cr-paper:#fff3e5   --cr-black-soft:#2d2926   --cr-white:#ffffff
--cr-red:#ff4001     --cr-pink:#fb66e5         --cr-yellow:#ffc72c
--cr-green:#009e50   --cr-green-light:#a9ee65  --cr-olive:#426130
--cr-teal:#00c2b9    --cr-gold:#feb002         --cr-orange:#ff5800

--br-xs:4px  --br-sm:8px  --br-md:14px  --br-lg:24px  --br-xl:40px
--s-gap-xs:4px --s-gap-sm:8px --s-gap-md:16px --s-gap-lg:24px --s-gap-xl:36px
--frame-width:6px
```

and its emphasis idiom, repeated across four hues:

```
box-shadow: 0 0 0 var(--frame-width) var(--cr-yellow),
            0 0 0 calc(var(--frame-width)*2) var(--cr-red);
```

**Two findings.** First, **stacked flat colour rings** stand in for elevation — visually this is
riso *misregistration*, the halo you get when two plates don't line up. Second, and against the
grain of Zinely's own V2 conclusions: Maeve **does** publish a radius scale and a spacing scale, and
they are small, memorable and actually used (`4/8/14/24/40`, `4/8/16/24/36`).

## 3. The common grammar

✅ **VERIFIED — all three share five moves**, independently:

1. **A warm ground and a soft brown-black ink.** None uses white paper or true black. `#f7e9d0` ·
   `#FFFCD5` · `#fff3e5`, against `#512d1e` · `#442619` · `#2d2926`.
2. **Saturated accents used as flat blocks**, never as gradients.
3. **A deliberately imperfect display face over a clean body sans.**
4. **Hard, un-blurred depth** — an offset shadow or a stacked ring, never a soft Material shadow.
5. **Pill geometry on controls**, soft-round on cards.

🟨 **ASSUMPTION** — a sixth move (tape, torn edges, stickers, rotation) is present in Nic's markup by
keyword and is visually characteristic of the category, but the mechanical sweep cannot confirm how
each asset is used without rendering the pages. Treat the specific craft motifs as proposals to be
judged in the prototype, not as verified reference practice.

## 4. Where this leaves Zinely's V2

Stated plainly, because the re-freeze should not be sold as a bigger change than it is:

**V2 is already in this family.** It has the warm paper (`#F7F2E7`), the soft brown-black ink
(`#2A251E`), a riso grain overlay, paper stock with a cut edge and a bound spine, and a muted
three-hue palette. What V2 does *not* have is the **loudness** — it is a quiet, well-mannered riso,
and the references are a loud, printed, slightly wonky one.

The concrete distance, measured:

| | Frozen V2 | The references |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | soft blurred shadows, 25 distinct declarations | hard offsets and flat rings, zero blur |
| Radius | 16 distinct chrome radii, 2–22px, no scale | a published 4/8/14/24/40 scale + pills |
| Display face | Fraunces 500 — a refined contemporary serif | an *averaged-handwriting* or schoolbook-print face |
| Accents | 3 muted hues (matcha, strawberry, consequence) | 4–8 saturated hues used as blocks |
| Geometry | everything square to the grid | objects rest at an angle |
| Craft motifs | none | tape, stickers, stamps, torn edges |

🟦 **RECOMMENDATION.** The re-freeze should be understood as **turning V2's own volume up**, not as
discarding it. Its paper metaphor, its privacy-first voice, its "every screen answers one question"
structure and its four-state discipline are all worth keeping; what changes is depth, geometry,
display type and the presence of craft motifs.

## 6. Open questions for the owner

1. **The display face.** Averia Sans Libre is OFL-licensed and free to bundle, but adding it is a
   fourth bundled family (Inter, Fraunces ×3 cuts, + Averia) and a real APK-size and
   font-coverage decision. Bundle it, or reach the handmade voice with Fraunces alone?
2. **The fourth hue.** V2-TOKENS' "no fourth chrome hue" rule was load-bearing for two accepted ADRs
   and a lint wall. Does V2.1 keep three hues, or publish four?
3. **Scales.** D-006 and D-007 ruled that V2 publishes no radius or spacing scale because the frozen
   corpus had none. V2.1's corpus can be *authored* with one. Publish scales, or keep values at the
   component?
4. **How much of V2 survives.** Are the Bench and Proof re-frozen too, or is the Library a pilot?

None of these is answered *here* — research states the trade, it does not rule on it. **All four are now
carried, with a recommendation and the reasoning against each, in
[V21-SPEC.md §3](V21-SPEC.md#3-the-four-rulings-this-document-asks-for)**, and put to the owner as
[ADR-099](../DECISIONS.md#adr-099) (`Proposed`). In short: bundle the face but subset it · keep the
no-fourth-chrome-hue rule and classify butter as *material* rather than chrome · publish both scales, because
V2.1's corpus was authored with them where V2's was not · freeze all three surfaces and no more.

## 7. Fold-diagram conventions — ✅ VERIFIED

Researched **2026-08-09**, after the owner rejected the Proof's first fold arrows as *messy*. The finding is
that this problem was solved long before Zinely: origami diagrams have an international standard, the
**Yoshizawa–Randlett system**, introduced by Akira Yoshizawa in *Atarashi Origami Geijutsu* (1954) and set out
for practitioners by [Robert Lang](https://langorigami.com/article/origami-diagramming-conventions/).

The rules that mattered here:

- **A motion arrow's tail is as important as its head.** Lang: *"the tail indicates precisely the reference
  point or spot undergoing motion. If you are to bring point A to point B, the tail of the arrow will touch
  [A]."* Our arrows floated beside the paper — which is precisely why they read as decoration.
- **Motion arrows have a plain single-line stem and no tail decoration.** A cleft or decorated tail makes the
  reference point ambiguous (Lang, fig. 8).
- **Action arrows have a hollow stem** — push, pull, inflate — so *"this paper travels"* can never be misread
  as *"do this to the paper"* (fig. 7).
- **Unfolding takes a double-headed hollow arrow**, and the same symbol whether it unfolds in one step or two
  (fig. 13).
- **Valley folds are dashed lines** — *"no ifs, ands, or buts"* — and should begin and end on a dash so the
  fold's endpoints are unambiguous (fig. 5).

🟦 **RECOMMENDATION.** Adopt the convention wholesale for every Zinely fold or assembly diagram rather than
inventing a house style. It is older than the app, internationally understood, and it had already solved the
failure we shipped. Landed as a binding rule in [V21-SPEC.md §5.3](V21-SPEC.md).

## 8. Two questions the freeze gate raised — researched 2026-08-09

Both came out of [V21-SPEC.md §8](V21-SPEC.md) rows 7 and 8, which an independent review opened and the owner
sent back to be researched and fixed rather than ruled on blind.

### 8.1 Does a border have to clear 3:1? — ✅ VERIFIED, and the answer is *"only when it identifies a control"*

[WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast) requires 3:1 for *"visual information
required to identify **user interface components** and states"*, where a
[user interface component](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-user-interface-components) is *"a part of the
content that is perceived by users as a **single control** for a distinct function"*.

[Eric Eggert's deep-dive](https://yatil.net/blog/non-text-contrast-in-detail-ui-components) supplies the test
that matters here — the question is not *"is there a border?"* but *"is the border **required** to identify
the thing?"*. He works it with a login form and reaches the opposite conclusion to the intuitive one: with
placeholders present, quoting Patrick H. Lauke, *"there's no hard need for the border to identify/perceive the
presence of the field"*; remove them and *"guesswork like this means that the visual information required to
identify the UI component is missing"* — and only then does the border become binding. He also kills a common
over-reading: the border need **not** clear 3:1 against *both* its inside and its outside — *"adjacent
colour(s)"* means the colours it actually abuts.

**What this settles for V2.1.** A decorative card frame is not a control and its border is not binding. So the
61 borders were **never a blanket 1.4.11 failure**, and "repaint them all for accessibility" would have been
the wrong reason to do the right thing. 🟦 The correct reason is design consistency, below.

### 8.2 How does a hard-shadow language behave in dark? — ✅ VERIFIED: the border inverts, the shadow does not

The category V2.1 borrows its depth from has a settled convention.
[NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/) and the
[neubrutalism reference](https://neubrutalism.com/) both describe the same two-property core — a hard border
plus a zero-blur offset shadow — and on dark grounds **the border inverts to the light ink while the shadow
stays dark**. The two properties are not the same colour and were never meant to be: a shadow is the absence
of light and must stay darker than the surface, whereas a drawn line is *ink* and must stay legible on it.

**V2.1 had collapsed both onto one token.** `inkLine` is `#120E0A` in dark — correct for a printed shadow,
and it put **61 borders** (8 Library · 19 Proof · 34 Bench) at **1.38:1 on `paper`** and the Proof's fold-sheet
outline at 1.38:1 against its own
fill. 🟦 **A border follows `ink`; only a shadow follows `inkLine`.** Because `ink` and `inkLine` are the same
`#33261C` in light, the correction is a **zero-pixel change in light theme** and a full inversion in dark —
which is exactly what the reference convention prescribes. Landed in [V21-SPEC.md §4.3](V21-SPEC.md).

### 8.3 The spacing scale — ⚠️ DISPUTED, then measured, and the corpus lost the argument

§2.3 praised Maeve's published `4/8/16/24/36`, and [V21-SPEC.md §3.3](V21-SPEC.md) claimed V2.1's corpus was
*"authored with a scale from the first line"*. **It was not.** Measured across all three prototypes: **281
spacing declarations, 30 distinct values**, a near-continuous ramp from 1 to 18 px — `--gap-*` was declared in
one prototype and **used nowhere**.

Fitting confirmed there was no latent scale to discover: a brute-forced best-fit 7-step ladder comes out as
`2/5/8/11/14/18/28`, which is a description of noise, not a scale. So the honest options were to withdraw the
claim or to **make it true**, and the owner chose to make it true:

| Candidate | mean movement | within 2 px |
|---|---|---|
| the published `4/8/16/24/36` | 1.64 px | 76.4% |
| `+12` → `4/8/12/16/24/36` | 1.11 px | 95.3% |
| **chosen: `2/4/8/12/16/24/36`** | **0.94 px** | **97.1%** |
| brute-forced best fit `2/5/8/11/14/18/28` | 0.90 px | — |

🟦 The chosen ladder is Maeve's, plus `12` where the corpus genuinely clustered and a `2 px` hairline step it
could not do without. It buys 97.1% coverage for **0.06 px** more average movement than the unmemorable
best fit. **273 declarations now route through it**; values above 40 px are one-off layout dimensions, not
rhythm, and stay literal.
