Why bother with paper

Some things are better held than scrolled.

  • It ends.

    Eight pages, then you are done. A finished thing, not a feed that wants more from you tomorrow.

  • It stays yours.

    No account, no upload, no audience counter. Your pages stay on your phone until you decide to share them.

  • It travels.

    Hand it to a friend, post it, leave it on a café table. Or keep it as a PDF and read it on your phone.

What people make with eight pages

Enough room for almost anything small.

Photos, a few words, and a piece or two of Art. Some ideas to start from:

  • A trip in eight photos
  • A recipe from someone you miss
  • A birthday booklet with the inside jokes left in
  • A field guide to one very specific street
  • A flyer that folds into a tiny book
  • A letter too long for a card

How to fold a mini zine.

One printed sheet, a pair of scissors, and a few good creases. This is where the software becomes stationery.

Start with: your Zinely PDF printed on one side of A4 or US Letter paper. A blank sheet works for practice, too.

Fold lengthways

Motion starts only when you ask. Pause at any point.

Made by you. Ready to pass on.

  1. 1Fold lengthways

    Lay the sheet landscape, printed side down.Bring the bottom long edge up to the top edge.Crease firmly and keep it folded.

  2. 2Fold in half again

    Keep the long strip lying across your desk.Bring its right short edge over to the left.Press the new fold flat.

  3. 3One more fold

    Fold the small rectangle in half once more.Bring the right edge over to the left.Press all the creases firmly.

  4. 4Open it all out

    Unfold the sheet completely.Lay it landscape again.Check for eight panels: four across, two down.

  5. 5Fold across the middle

    Bring the full sheet’s right short edge over to the left.This fold goes the other way from your first fold.Keep the folded edge on your right.

  6. 6Make one small cut

    Start at the folded right edge; cut left along the middle crease.Cut through both layers, stopping halfway across.Stop at the dot. Do not cut all the way through.

  7. 7Open up again

    Unfold the sheet and lay it flat.The slit crosses only the two middle panels.Both outer panels must stay joined.

  8. 8Return to the long fold

    Fold lengthways again along the first crease.Keep the printed pages on the outside.Hold the strip with the slit along its folded top edge.

  9. 9Push the ends together

    Hold the short ends and gently push them inward.Let the slit open into a diamond.Keep pushing until four panels meet in a cross.

  10. 10Fold into a little book

    Wrap the panels around each other, front cover on top.Flatten the spine with your fingers.Your eight-page zine is ready to share.

What makes it different

Imperfect surface. Perfect mechanics.

The page can be as crooked, dotty, and handmade as you like. The parts that decide whether the zine works are not left to chance.

Loose where you want it

  • Resize a photo, crop it close, or flip it.
  • Give it the photocopier look: dots, contrast, and no pretending it is not a copy.
  • Add Art and a few words, then move everything again later.

Exact where it matters

  • All eight pages land in the right place, the right way up, once folded.
  • The PDF is made at A4 or US Letter size, ready to print at 100%.
  • One sheet, one cut. The fold guide shows every step.

Private without trying

  • Works offline. The app does not ask for internet access at all.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics.
  • Your photos are copied into the app’s private storage and never uploaded.

About Zinely

Apparently, we had to make it ourselves.

Sometimes you want to make something you can hand to someone. A few photos, a short story, a small obsession that apparently needed its own publication.

We’re Aastra, a two-person team, and we wanted that too. We went looking for an app that would help us make little paper zines without making a project of the page order. We couldn’t find quite what we wanted, so we started building Zinely.

What started on a screen ends up in someone’s hands. That’s the part we wanted to get to.

We handle the page order. You decide what deserves eight pages.
